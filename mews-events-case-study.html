Knowledge migration for Mews Events

This case study reframes Mews Events documentation as a knowledge migration problem: moving from fragmented, feature-led information toward a clearer, workflow-based model that supports users, support teams, and future maintenance.

Knowledge migration Information architecture Documentation strategy

Why this matters

  • The core issue was structural, not only editorial. Users needed guidance that matched real tasks, not a scattered set of product explanations.
  • The work had migration value. It translated implicit product knowledge into a more explicit, reusable, and maintainable documentation model.
  • The output supports scale. A better content structure helps documentation stay usable as workflows, ownership, and features evolve.

Context

A documentation problem shaped by cross-functional workflows.

Mews Events sits in a product area where operational steps, commercial decisions, and system behaviour intersect. That makes the documentation challenge less about writing isolated articles and more about organising knowledge in a way that reflects how people actually work.

Task

Turn complex event-related product knowledge into guidance that is easier to navigate, interpret, and maintain.

Scope

Documentation structure, terminology, workflow clarity, and the relationship between feature content and user outcomes.

Challenge

The issue was fragmented knowledge, not simply missing content.

In product areas like Mews Events, users rarely think in terms of isolated features. They think in tasks, decisions, exceptions, and handoffs. When documentation is organised too closely around product surfaces, users often struggle to find the right entry point.

Symptoms in the knowledge model

  • Information can become fragmented across related topics and support scenarios.
  • Terminology may drift between product language, internal language, and user-facing guidance.
  • Feature-oriented pages can make workflow understanding harder than it needs to be.
  • Users may depend on support or tribal knowledge to bridge missing context.

Why this matters operationally

  • Documentation becomes harder to trust when users cannot predict where an answer will live.
  • Maintenance cost rises when overlapping explanations live in multiple places.
  • Support load increases when important context is implicit rather than documented.
  • Future product change becomes harder to communicate cleanly.

Migration approach

Restructure the knowledge, not just the wording.

I would position this work as documentation architecture with a migration lens: understanding the current knowledge landscape, identifying where users lose context, and reshaping the content model so guidance follows the logic of real work.

1. Audit the current state

Review existing articles, user questions, edge cases, terminology, and product flows to identify overlap, gaps, and ambiguous entry points.

2. Reframe around workflows

Shift the structure from feature-led explanations toward task-led guidance, so users can move through event-related decisions with less friction.

3. Align terminology

Reduce ambiguity across labels, concepts, and support-facing language so the same idea is described consistently throughout the experience.

4. Support future maintenance

Build a structure that scales better over time, with clearer ownership boundaries, easier updates, and less duplicated explanation.

What changed

A better documentation model makes the product easier to learn and support.

Even without presenting private internal metrics, the value of this work can be shown by the shift in structure, findability, and maintainability between an older knowledge state and a clearer target model.

Before and after

Before Feature-oriented content, scattered explanations, and harder-to-predict navigation paths.
After Workflow-oriented guidance with clearer grouping, stronger entry points, and more explicit context.
Before Terminology and mental models can vary between teams, features, and support conversations.
After Documentation supports a more stable language model and reduces the gap between product behaviour and user understanding.
Before Knowledge is harder to maintain when related explanations are distributed across overlapping pages.
After A clearer structure creates stronger conditions for ownership, updates, reuse, and long-term governance.
